Looped models think more per weight. They don't know more.

Three looped models launched within a month. A looped model runs some of its layers more than once per token, with the same weights, so it does more computation without storing more. We measured two of them on our factual test, and turned one model's loop count like a dial. Extra depth moved caution, not knowledge.

By Mark Galyan. Every number links to a receipt.

The test

240 short factual questions: 40 easy, 100 hard, and 100 about things that do not exist, with UNKNOWN offered as an answer. Greedy decoding, the same build and machine for every pair compared. "Declined" means the model answered UNKNOWN; "made up" means it answered a question about something that does not exist.

1. Turning the dial: one model at 1, 2 and 3 loops

Measured

SenseNova-Loop-9B was trained to run its middle 24 layers twice. We rewrote only the loop count in the model file, checked each version against SenseTime's own code (within 0.3 % on Wikipedia perplexity), and asked the same 240 questions at each setting.

A third loop declined 14 more invented questions, with answers no longer than before: the extra thinking happened inside the network, not on the page. But it also got 5 fewer hard facts right, and ran 30 % slower. One loop broke the model outright (8 of 40 easy questions right, against 27). Receipt.

2. A looped model built from an ordinary one

Measured

SenseNova-Loop-9B is Qwen3.5-9B underneath: every layer's weights still match Qwen's (cosine 0.97; unrelated layers score 0.00), and its vision part is identical. Then it was retrained hard, moving the median weight by a quarter of its size. Against Qwen3.5-9B on the same machine:

Qwen3.5-9B654236 / 40

A third of the hard facts. Its Wikipedia perplexity is twice Qwen's, and SenseTime's own code confirms it, so this is the model, not our setup. Its card puts it level with Qwen3.8-27B on several agent benchmarks: strong at tasks, weak at trivia. Receipt, weight comparison.

3. Inkling-2-Mini against Qwen3.8-27B

Measured

Inkling-2-Mini (26B mixture-of-experts, middle layers run three times) cannot switch its reasoning off; its lowest setting still thinks briefly. So we ran Qwen3.8-27B both ways, on the same two-GPU machine and build:

ModelInvented declinedInvented made upHard rightDecode speed
Qwen3.8-27B, thinking off59394810.8 tok/s
Qwen3.8-27B, brief thinking88124410.8 tok/s
Inkling-2-Mini, lowest setting7863315.6 tok/s

Its card warns that it states wrong things with confidence more than larger models. On invented questions it made up the fewest answers of the three. But matched for reasoning, Qwen declined more and knew more: Inkling's caution came mostly from its reasoning. It is faster per token; its slowest answers were reasoning loops that never ended under greedy decoding (22 of 240). Receipt, matched run.

What it adds up to

Reading

Loops buy computation, not stored facts. Facts live in the weights; reading the same weights twice does not add any. Both looped models know less than comparable ordinary models, and GLM-5.4's own published comparison of a looped and an unlooped 30B model (their blog) gained most on math and science reasoning (+11 and +9 points) and little on the knowledge-heavy MMLU-Pro (+3).

The loop count can work like a caution dial, moving "I don't know" without spending extra words. On the one model we could turn, that caution was not free: it also cost facts.

Reasoning is still the bigger lever. About 200 tokens of thinking moved Qwen3.8-27B from 59 to 88 invented questions declined, at almost no cost in facts.

If you run them locally

  • llama.cpp (mainline, 24 November) splits Inkling-2-Mini across GPUs by loop slots (48) rather than stored layers (24), so an even split puts every layer on the first card. On two cards, -ts 1,3 balances it.
  • Inkling's lowest setting still reasons, and greedy decoding can trap that reasoning in a loop; its makers sample at temperature 1.0.
  • SenseNova support was still an open pull request; one of its commits reuses the first pass's cache on the second pass, unlike SenseTime's code, and changed 209 of 240 answers.
  • Extra passes cost speed on bandwidth-limited cards: each pass re-reads those layers' weights.

Limits

  • Two looped models, one test of short factual English questions, greedy decoding.
  • SenseNova was trained at 2 loops, so 1 and 3 are outside what it learned.
  • Inkling's lowest setting and Qwen's "brief thinking" are not the same instruction.
